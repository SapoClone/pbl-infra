What we have, and what is missing

At a glance

The backend is far ahead of everything else. It can serve most of the tenant web screens and all of the mobile screens today. The web app shows made-up data on four screens, has placeholders on five and nothing on eight; the mobile app does not exist yet. Three feature areas have no backend at all: platform admin, marketplace channels and email flows.

Decisions that need people

  1. Which API shape does the frontend build against? The contracts written for the Figma screens (what main follows), or the shapes in the M1 to M6 documents. The comparison recommends the contracts: main matches most of each service's contract endpoints (20 of 21 for sales, 39 of 46 for inventory, 17 of 20 for catalog, 16 of 19 for customer), while the other work matches none of them exactly.
  2. Who ports what, and in which order from the M1 to M6 work into main: row-level security and events first, then inventory cost and receipts, then returns and price lists.
  3. When to switch to row-level security and per-service database roles. It needs an audit of the raw SQL first.
  4. Money and quantity types: integer VND as the repository rule says, or decimal strings as in the other work.
  5. Scope: are price lists, promotions, customer tiers, holds and the checkout saga needed now, or after the screens work?
  6. Merge pbl-infra #6 (the ERD changes for the design), and decide what happens to #5.

Risks to know about

In the unmerged M1 to M6 work

  • Review findings on this work, security among them, go to its author directly and are not listed on this public page.
  • Only the pull-request title check ran on all six pull requests; their tests are unverified.

On main

  • Tokens issued before the permissions change give non-owners a 403 until they sign in again.
  • The new role column is required: deploy the migration atomically, and reset any local database that has members.
  • Stock is keyed by SKU text, not by catalog variant, and reservations are made with the user's own token.
  • The audit log is not limited by branch, so a branch-restricted manager with report access sees every branch's entries.
