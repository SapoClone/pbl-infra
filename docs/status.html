At a glance

The backend is far ahead of everything else, and since 31 October it grew in three ways: all eight services now have real logic on main (the contributor work was adopted), the full stack is defined for AWS, and the platform admin API and the business rules of branches, warehouses, stock moves, cash shifts and plans are built and on main (store, plan, subscription, usage, account, admin and settings endpoints; plan limits and plan expiry; a default warehouse per branch; a branch guard; a cash drawer with counting and manager approval; stock integrity fixes; S3 image uploads). The admin API serves 12 of the 23 admin screens completely and 2 in part; monitoring, integrations, failed jobs, the audit log, notices and support sessions have no endpoint yet. The mobile app still does not exist, and the tenant-screen table below was last audited on 31 October. Email flows (invitation, password reset) are still missing.

Decisions that need people

  1. Plan prices and limits. The seed uses the contract's examples (free 1 branch, 3 users, 100 products, 1 GB; basic 299,000 VND; pro 599,000 VND). Those numbers are enforced now, and an expired plan drops to free after 7 days, so the team should confirm or change them in the admin.

Give them back their remaining branches, ask the owner to reassign, or leave them: the system cannot decide, because removing them would turn those members into "every branch" members.
  3. Payment confirmation at the till. A card or QR payment is confirmed on the cashier's word (a reference is required and who confirmed is recorded). Decide whether it must be verified with the payment provider before the sale completes.
  4. Platform-scope audit. Admin actions that belong to no store (creating an admin, changing settings, ending an account's sessions) need their own audit design before they can emit events.
  5. Two-factor sign-in for platform admins before production use.

Risks to know about

To do when deploying the new code

  • Apply pbl-infra #14 and #15 first (image bucket access, plan and status events, the branch guard URLs); catalog and platform are event consumers, and the platform calls inventory, sales and integration when a branch is deactivated.
  • Rebuild the catalog image (it gains two AWS packages) and run the plan resync once after catalog's projection is deployed, or stores older than the limits stay unlimited in catalog.
  • Nothing ran against real S3, a browser upload through the bucket's CORS rule, or CloudFront.
  • A store suspended before sales and integration got the status copy counts as active there until its next status event.

On main

  • A role or permission change reaches a member at their next sign-in or refresh.
  • The auth endpoints forgot-password, reset-password and verify-email are still placeholders, and sign-up saves the account before the verification email is queued.
  • Inventory keys stock by warehouse and SKU (the adopted model), and calls made by jobs and consumers have no service token yet.
  • The audit log is not limited by branch, so a branch-restricted manager with report access sees every branch's entries.
  • No rate limiting.
